The most common mistake people make with a tiger reserve is treating the safari as a sight to see rather than a morning to live through. This is the second kind of guide: what actually happens, hour by hour, and every piece of admin you need — verified against the MP Forest Department's official portal.

## First, the paperwork (or how to skip it)

Ratapani runs **morning and evening safari rounds** from its two main safari gates, **Jhiri** and **Delawadi**, with permits issued on **forest.mponline.gov.in**. The structure, per the official portal:

- **Online quota per round is tiny** — at Jhiri, two full-vehicle permits and one single-seat permit; at Delawadi, one full-vehicle permit. The rest is DFO quota and at-gate availability.
- **Only registered safari vehicles** may enter at Jhiri and Delawadi. The **Bhimbetka gate** is the exception: offline booking, private vehicles allowed.
- **Official Jhiri fees:** ₹750 entry + ₹3,000 registered gypsy + ₹480 guide ≈ **₹4,230 per jeep**, seating up to six. Single seats: ₹125 per adult, ₹65 per child, plus vehicle share. Fees are revised by circular — check the portal before you travel.

Or skip all of it: resort guests hand the problem to our travel desk with their room booking. Permit, registered jeep, resident naturalist — held against your dates. This is, frankly, the strongest argument for [staying near the reserve](/blog/where-to-stay-near-ratapani) rather than day-tripping from a city hotel.

## The morning, hour by hour

**5:00 — the knock.** Coffee and biscuits at the door. In winter it's dark and 9°C; dress in layers you can shed. In summer this hour is the coolest the day will ever be.

**5:40 — the drive.** From the estate you're on the forest side of Bhopal already — no city to cross. The road empties, the headlights pick up nightjars on the tarmac, and the naturalist starts telling you what the forest did this week: which waterhole the tigress has been using, where the dhole pack crossed.

**6:15 — the gate.** Permits checked, guide boards, the barrier lifts. The first kilometres are the best argument for silence — teak forest going from black to grey to gold.

**6:30–9:30 — the round.** Ratapani is ~55% teak and a quarter bamboo, folded over the Vindhyan hills, and the track work is honest: ridge climbs, stream crossings, long contours above the Ratapani dam backwaters. What you're statistically most likely to see: chital and sambar constantly; langurs on every second ridge; nilgai and wild boar in the open country; sloth bear and leopard if the ridges are kind; and the reserve's roughly 90 tigers on their own schedule, not yours.

The naturalist's craft is the real show — reading alarm calls, aging a pugmark by its edge crispness, positioning the jeep where a calling chital says the cat will cross. Learn to enjoy that and you will never have a "failed" safari again.

**9:45 — out, and hungry.** Back through the gate as the heat rises and the forest goes quiet. This is where proximity pays a second time: while day-trippers face the drive back to town, you're twenty minutes from a [farm breakfast](/blog/inside-royalton-farms) — eggs from the estate's hens, poha, hand-pressed coffee — and then a plunge pool.

## What to bring

| Season | Essentials |
|---|---|
| Winter (Nov–Feb) | Windproof jacket, beanie, gloves — 8–10°C feels arctic in a moving jeep |
| Summer (Apr–Jun) | Full sleeves, hat, 2L water, electrolytes — it will pass 40°C by ten |
| Always | Binoculars (one pair per two people), sunscreen, a camera you already know how to use |

Leave at home: bright clothing, perfume, and the expectation of a tiger. The forest doesn't do appointments.

## Evening safaris

The evening round runs the golden hours to sunset — softer light, active waterholes in summer, and a different cast (this is when sloth bears tend to move). If you're staying two nights, do one of each; the forest at 7 a.m. and the forest at 5:30 p.m. are different places.
