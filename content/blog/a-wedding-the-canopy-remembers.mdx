A wedding here is not held in a hall. It is spread across an estate, and the estate remembers it.

The mehndi greens gather by the lily pond. The haldi happens under a marigold canopy in the mango grove. The sangeet fills a pillarless hall lit by a thousand bulbs, the chairs lifting away from a dance floor. And the pheras — those wait for the lake at dusk, four fires under an old tamarind tree, the bride’s side and the groom’s meeting on the lawn.

## A venue for each hour

The estate holds eleven venues — five pillarless indoor halls and six lawns and lakesides — so each function finds the room it deserves rather than being squeezed into one. The Aria Grand seats the largest gatherings; the lakeside deck seats twenty. We size the team to your guest list, not the other way around.

> The flowers are grown a few steps from where they go on the mandap. Nothing is flown in to fade by Sunday.

## One planner, your wedding only

There is one named human — on email, on phone — from the proposal to the vidaai. Not a desk that gets passed around. Decor from Royalton Farms, live food stations, branded buffets, and rooms on-site for every guest so the family stays under one roof.

The forest does the rest. It always has.
